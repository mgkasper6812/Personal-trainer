# Youth Athlete S&C / Training-Delivery Platforms: Competitive Teardown and Youth-Safety Practice

Research date: 2026-11-20. Method note: the session's network blocked direct page fetches (teambuildr.com, trainheroic.com, en.wikipedia.org all failed DNS), so findings come from web-search result summaries of official pages, help centers, App Store listings, press releases, review sites and peer-reviewed papers. Claims taken only from third-party price aggregators are flagged. Anything not sourced is listed under Gaps or labelled as an inference.

## 2. Athlete-side daily UX (workout card, video, logging, readiness questionnaires, compliance, streaks)

### Takeaway
The standard pattern is: a short readiness survey (about 5 items on 1–5 scales: sleep, mood/stress, energy/fatigue, soreness), then today's workout with exercise demo video and set/rep/load logging, then a "finish" tap that feeds compliance. Volt adds AI auto-adjustment from athlete feedback. Teamworks AMS and TeamBuildr AMS let coaches build custom questionnaires.

### Cited Findings
- **TrainHeroic** has a pre-training readiness survey with questions on sleep, mood, energy, stress and soreness. Responses are averaged into a 1–5 readiness score. It is "the 5-question readiness survey that all TrainHeroic athletes get" — [TrainHeroic support](https://support.trainheroic.com/hc/en-us/articles/18156547276557); [TrainHeroic blog: readiness surveys](https://trainheroic.com/?p=3495)
- A third-party summary lists recent TrainHeroic updates: nutrition tracking, exercise swaps, and rep ranges with RIR (reps in reserve) — [checkthat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/trainheroic) (unverified)
- **Volt**: "Cortex AI" adapts training based on athlete feedback and performance. Workouts run in the Volt Training App (phone or tablet) and can also be printed or shown on weight-room screens — [Volt HS pricing page](https://voltathletics.com/pricing-high-schools/)
- **TeamBuildr**: coaches build questions in a "Sports Science" module, group them into questionnaires and attach them to workouts. One coach's "Ready to Work" questionnaire includes how the body feels physically and overall soreness or pain level — [TeamBuildr blog](https://blog.teambuildr.com/posts/using-teambuildr-create-ready-work-assessment). The AMS add-on covers pain, soreness and load monitoring, including athlete-reported pain/soreness tagged to body parts — [TeamBuildr AMS](https://www.teambuildr.com/platform-ams); [TeamBuildr downloads](https://teambuildr.com/downloads)
- **BridgeAthletic/BridgeTracker**: athletes "complete an in-app readiness survey… prior to your training session" — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1024299963). A separate Wellness Form ("assess overall well-being") lands in the coach web platform's forms module — [BridgeAthletic help](https://intercom.help/bridgeathletic/en/articles/8949299-can-i-assign-forms-to-athletes)
- **Teamworks AMS**: configurable wellness/readiness questionnaires that can also pull in data from "the wearable of your choice" — [Teamworks AMS](https://teamworks.com/ams/)
- **CoachNow** is a skill-coaching and video app rather than an S&C program-delivery app. It offers a video/image analysis suite, voice-over ("CoachCam"), 240 fps capture, angle tools, and AI skeleton/joint-angle tracking (shown in some locales) — [CoachNow help](https://help.coachnow.io/en/articles/8534029-what-s-the-price-of-coachnow-analyze); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id596598472); [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/coachnow/reviews)
- **Gatorade Gx**: a single-use forearm sweat patch, scanned by phone, estimates sweat rate and sodium concentration. The free Gx app covers workouts, nutrition and sleep and pulls in Apple Health, Strava and Garmin data. No youth-specific features or age guidance were found — [Gatorade Gx App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1525554148); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/gatorades-gx-sweat-patch-delivers-hydration-fitness-guidance-athletes-phones)
- **Science behind the survey format**: the Hooper/McLean-style wellness questionnaire usually uses 1–5 scales across fatigue, sleep, soreness, stress and mood — [Frontiers in Psychology](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.671072/xml). A 2020 systematic review of 21 studies found single-item and composite wellness measures related to training load "ranging from no association to a very large association." Fatigue and general muscle soreness respond most to load — [PMC7534939](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7534939). Reliability and meaningful-change thresholds are poorly established — [UTS AFL measurement study](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/142301/2/Measurement%20characteristics%20of%20athlete%20monitoring%20tools%20in%20professional%20Australian%20football%20%282%29.pdf). A bad score may come from life stress rather than training — [World Rugby Passport](https://passport.world.rugby/conditioning-for-rugby/introduction-to-conditioning-adult/managing-the-training-plan/monitoring-the-training-load/wellness-questionnaires/)

### Inferences
- **Pattern to copy:** a 5-item, 1–5 check-in (sleep quality, energy/fatigue, general soreness, mood, stress), taking under 30 seconds, placed as a gate before today's workout card. Add a **separate** pain item with a body-map location (as in TeamBuildr AMS) rather than folding pain into "soreness."
- **Pattern to copy:** let the athlete use the plan on a phone or a printout (Volt). Teens train in school weight rooms and at practice.
- **Anti-pattern:** averaging everything into one "readiness" number (TrainHeroic's 1–5 average) hides a single alarming item such as pain 4/5 on the throwing shoulder. Show the composite score, but flag individual items too.
- Compare each athlete with her own baseline rather than with fixed population cut-offs. This is an inference from the poorly established reliability and meaningful-change thresholds.
- For a softball player, the app could add CoachNow-style video upload for throwing and hitting mechanics. That is a differentiator, since S&C apps don't do sport-skill video well.

### Gaps
- Streaks, leaderboards and gamification in TeamBuildr, Volt and TrainHeroic were not confirmed in retrieved sources. TeamBuildr is known for "leaderboards" and TrainHeroic for team leaderboards, but neither was verified here.
- Exact question wording and scale anchors for Volt's athlete feedback were not found.

## 3. Coach/parent dashboards, alerts and flag triggers

### Takeaway
Coach dashboards show color-coded questionnaire reports, compliance (workouts completed or opened), PRs and pain/soreness by body part. Alerts are mostly configurable flags (Teamworks AMS, CoachMePlus outliers) or email digests (TeamBuildr). **None of the platforms surveyed advertises a parent-specific view.** A parent dashboard with pain alerts is an open niche.

### Cited Findings
- **TeamBuildr**: questionnaire data is "instantly sent to the coach dashboard and color-coded reports" — [TeamBuildr sport science](https://teambuildr.com/sport-science). Dashboards show trends and areas of athlete-reported pain/soreness by body part — [TeamBuildr downloads](https://teambuildr.com/downloads). Coaches can "automatically send email notifications to coaches to check their teams' report" — [TeamBuildr systems PDF](https://www.teambuildr.com/hubfs/Systems%20Based%20Approach%20to%20using%20TeamBuildr%20(1).pdf). No documentation was found on soreness threshold flags.
- **Teamworks AMS**: monitors player and squad wellness, sets alerts and flags, and analyses change and variation — [Teamworks AMS for rugby](https://explore.teamworks.com/ams-for-rugby-teams). It unifies Medical, Performance and Coaching around "readiness, injury risk, and return-to-play," with injury-surveillance statistics — [Teamworks AMS](https://teamworks.com/ams/). A secondary guide says alerts can fire on missed check-ins, unusual responses, restrictions or milestones — [SportsFirst guide](https://www.sportsfirst.net/post/what-is-teamworks-athlete-management-system-a-complete-guide-for-us-sports-organizations) (secondary)
- **CoachMePlus** (comparable AMS) highlights outliers with color-coded alerts — [CoachMePlus](https://coachmeplus.com/underused-tool-in-sports-performance/)
- **BridgeAthletic**: wellness form results appear in the coach-side forms module — [BridgeAthletic help](https://intercom.help/bridgeathletic/en/articles/8949299-can-i-assign-forms-to-athletes). The coach app can view athlete feeds and invite athletes by QR code — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1024299963)
- **Compliance pitfall, TeamBuildr**: if athletes don't tap "finished," the workout looks as if it was never opened — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/teambuildr.com?page=2)
- **Parent visibility**: searches of TeamBuildr reviews found nothing aimed at parents. The platforms' feedback is from coaches, trainers and athletes — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/teambuildr.com)

### Inferences
- **Pattern to copy (from AMS tools):** use rule-based flags with clear triggers, including:
  - pain reported (any non-zero pain in a joint, or pain ≥ 3/10)
  - pain in the same location on 2 or more consecutive days
  - soreness rising day over day
  - a drop in the athlete's sleep or energy score from her own baseline
  - a missed check-in

  Send the parent a plain-language reason ("Maicie reported right-shoulder pain 4/10 after throwing; today's plan was changed to lower-body + mobility").
- **Pattern to copy:** a color-coded daily status (green/amber/red) plus a body-map heat view (TeamBuildr AMS).
- **Anti-pattern:** compliance that depends on a "finish" tap. Infer completion from logged sets, and prompt gently.
- **Anti-pattern:** overwhelming parents with raw numbers. Parents need actionable alerts, not ACWR charts.
- **Privacy design point (inference):** decide which fields the parent sees. For example, show pain and injury flags and plan adherence, but treat mood/stress free text and menstrual-cycle data as athlete-controlled. Otherwise teens may under-report.

### Gaps
- Default alert thresholds in any platform (e.g., "soreness ≥ 4 triggers a flag") were not documented publicly.
- Volt's coach dashboard specifics were not retrieved.

## 4. Pain vs soreness, return to play, throwing-arm care for softball position players, injury-report workflows

### Takeaway
Clinical guidance draws a clear line. Soreness that fades in about 1–3 days is normal. Pain that is sharp, starts during activity, is tied to a specific movement (such as throwing), or changes mechanics means stop and get evaluated. Softball has **no official pitch or throw count**, and softball-specific injury-prevention evidence is thin. Baseball-derived limits and "throwing volume is the #1 risk factor" are the working references.

### Cited Findings
- Delayed-onset soreness shows up a day or two after training and should last no longer than a couple of days. Pain that makes you limp, changes your throwing motion or forces compensation is a reason to stop — [Baptist Health](https://baptisthealth.net/baptist-health-news/sore-or-injured-how-to-know-when-to-play-through-the-pain-and-when-to-sit-out); [Duke Health](https://www.dukehealth.org/blog/no-pain-no-gain-not-best-medicine)
- High school athletes should never play through pain caused by a specific movement, but can work through muscle soreness to some extent. Pain every time a child throws warrants evaluation — [YourTeen](https://yourteenmag.com/sports/high-school-sports-injuries)
- Severe muscle pain with weakness, swelling or dark urine after hard exercise means stop and seek prompt care (rhabdomyolysis). Normal-colored urine does not rule it out — [CHOC](https://health.choc.org/rhabdomyolysis-in-young-athletes/)
- Year-round single-sport athletes often grit through aching for weeks, with parents or coaches dismissing it as "growing pains" — [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/health-news/child-health/growing-pains-for-young-athletes-it-might-be-a-sports-injury)
- In softball position players, overhead shoulder (and sometimes elbow) problems predominate. Recommended measures: warm up with running and easy, gradual throwing, and stop when velocity or accuracy drops, as an early sign of arm fatigue. Don't throw through shoulder or elbow pain, and see a doctor if pain lasts about a week — [Nicklaus Children's (STOP Sports Injuries)](https://www.nicklauschildrens.org/patient-resources/health-and-safety-information/softball-stop-sports-injuries)
- A systematic review found only one study of a softball injury-prevention program, and it was generic. There is no official softball pitch-count limit. Volume of throwing is the number one risk factor — [PMC11299331](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11299331)
- Youth baseball tables are the usual reference. NCYS maximum pitches: 95 for ages 13–16. Pitch Smart sets daily limits and rest days by age. Take at least 3 months off competitive throwing each year — [AAOS OrthoInfo](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/staying-healthy/baseball-injury-prevention); [SportsEngine/NCYS](https://discover.sportsengineplay.com/safety/baseball/baseball-injuries-tips-sheet/) (baseball, not softball)
- Platforms: Teamworks AMS links medical and performance data for injury surveillance and return-to-play — [Teamworks AMS](https://teamworks.com/ams/). No youth S&C app was found publishing explicit return-to-play logic.

### Inferences
- **App rules to implement (derived from the above, not from any platform):**
  - Ask for pain separately from soreness, with location, a 0–10 rating, "sharp vs achy," and "does it hurt when you throw?"
  - Any throwing-arm pain during or after throwing, any pain that changes mechanics, or any pain at the same site lasting more than about 5–7 days should do three things:
    1. remove throwing and overhead loading from that day's plan
    2. alert the parent
    3. recommend evaluation by an athletic trainer or physician
  - Red-flag symptoms (dark urine, swelling with weakness, numbness/tingling, pain at night or at rest) should trigger an urgent "see a clinician" message, not plan modification.
- **The AI should not make return-to-play decisions.** Model the process as clinician clearance, then a graded return, with the parent confirming clearance in the app.
- **Throwing-volume tracking:** a daily throw-count or "throwing load" log (practice + games + lessons) is a differentiator. Softball has no governing count, so present baseball-derived figures as reference only.
- Track total weekly team plus lesson plus S&C load, since athletes often have several coaches who don't communicate. This links to NATA's overuse guidance (section 5).

### Gaps
- Interval throwing programs for softball position players (as opposed to pitchers) were not retrieved.
- AAP clinical report on overuse injuries: referenced but not retrieved.

## 5. Youth S&C safety guidelines, load monitoring, RED-S and fueling, privacy for minors

### Takeaway
Supervised, well-designed resistance training is endorsed as safe and beneficial for youth (NSCA 2009, still the current statement). NATA recommends one organized sport per season, off-season breaks and delayed specialization. ACWR is scientifically contested, so use session-RPE trends and own-baseline monitoring without hard ACWR "danger zones." The 2023 IOC REDs consensus names adolescent females as an at-risk group and frames screening as a first step, not a diagnosis. Amended COPPA rules (compliance date April 22, 2026) apply only under 13. A 15–18-year-old app still needs teen-specific privacy care under state law (not researched here).

### Cited Findings
- **NSCA 2009** (Faigenbaum et al., JSCR) — [PubMed 19620931](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19620931/); [NSCA PDF](https://dxpprod.nsca.com/globalassets/about/position-statements/position_stand_youth_resistance_training---2009.pdf). It holds that a properly designed and supervised program:
  - is relatively safe
  - increases strength and power
  - improves cardiovascular risk profile and motor skills/sport performance
  - can increase resistance to sports injuries
  - improves psychosocial well-being and promotes lifelong exercise habits

  No newer NSCA youth statement was found. The NSCA also publishes parent-facing youth S&C guidance — [NSCA for parents](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/ptq/youth_performance_and_fitness_information_for_parents/)
- **NATA 2019 youth sport specialization statement** — [NATA PDF](https://www.nata.org/sites/default/files/youth_sports_specialization_recommendations.pdf); [NATA press release](https://www.nata.org/press-release/national-athletic-trainers-association-releases-official-statement-recommendations-reduce-risk). Recommendations:
  - delay single-sport year-round specialization
  - one organized sport per season
  - time away from organized sport at the end of each season
  - sample a variety of sports
  - The NATA pediatric overuse statement (ages 6–18) notes that overuse injuries are about 50% of pediatric sport injuries — [PMC3070508](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3070508)
- **ACWR critique**: Impellizzeri et al. (2021, Sports Medicine) argue that ACWR and its underlying theory should be dismissed, citing artefacts from how the ratio is built — [Impellizzeri 2021](https://iris.univr.it/retrieve/e34cfb98-e922-4c2f-aab5-18583ab7e31b/Impellizzeri_What%20Role%20Do%20Chronic%20Workloads%20Play_SportMed_2021.pdf). Further criticisms: inconsistent measurement and arbitrary time windows — [Frontiers editorial, PMC8138569](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138569). sRPE load (RPE × minutes) can be dominated by session duration — [Afonso et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2021.661200/pdf). Adult-validated load strategies cannot be directly extrapolated to youth — [youth soccer ACWR study](https://repositorio.ipsantarem.pt/bitstreams/d3905682-4323-48c8-9dd0-be1eba20f870/download)
- **IOC REDs 2023 consensus** — [PubMed 37752011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37752011/); [ACSM summary](https://acsm.org/hot-topic-energy-balance-a-key-component-to-female-athlete-well-being/):
  - published in BJSM 57(17):1073–1097
  - introduces the REDs Clinical Assessment Tool v2
  - screening via self-report questionnaires, health interviews and objective markers
  - adolescent female athletes named as an at-risk group
  - screening is a first step that informs clinical assessment and does not replace it
  - reported prevalence in female athletes ranges from 23% to 80% depending on definitions
- **COPPA**: amendments were published April 22, 2025 and took effect June 23, 2025. The general compliance deadline was April 22, 2026. They add written data-security-program and data-retention-policy requirements and new parental-consent methods — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2025/05/ftc-publishes-updates-to-coppa-rule); [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect). COPPA covers only children under 13 — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)

### Inferences
- **Load monitoring for the app:**
  - Collect session RPE (CR-10) after each session (S&C plus practice/games) and multiply by minutes.
  - Show weekly load and week-to-week change. Flag large jumps relative to her own history ("this week is much heavier than your usual").
  - Avoid presenting ACWR "sweet spot 0.8–1.3" claims as validated injury prediction.
- **RED-S-aware design:**
  - No weight tracking, calorie counting, body-composition or "lean"/"cut" language by default.
  - Frame fueling as "fuel for training": eat before and after sessions, carbohydrates on heavy days, hydration.
  - Include optional, private screening-style signals, such as recurring fatigue, frequent illness, missed or irregular periods, recurring bone pain/stress fractures, and dropping performance. These should lead to "talk to your doctor/athletic trainer," never to a diagnosis.
  - Keep menstrual data athlete-private unless she chooses to share it.
- **Sleep:** high schoolers need roughly 8–10 hours (AASM/AAP consensus; not retrieved in this session, verify). Use sleep check-in trends rather than wearable scores alone.
- **Youth resistance training:** emphasize technique, qualified supervision and gradual progression, consistent with NSCA. The AI plan should progress load only after technique is logged as solid, and include a coach or parent supervision note for heavy or new lifts.
- **Privacy:**
  - If the athlete is 13 or older, COPPA does not apply. Still use parental consent and account linking, data minimization and retention limits, and adopt the amended COPPA rule's written security and retention policies as best practice.
  - Health data entered in a consumer app is generally not covered by HIPAA. The FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and state consumer-health-data laws (e.g., Washington's My Health My Data Act) may apply. This is not verified in this session.

### Gaps
- Specific NSCA dosing (sets, reps, intensity for adolescents) was not retrieved. Verify from the NSCA PDF before quoting.
- ACSM youth resistance-training position content was not retrieved.
- State teen-privacy laws (CA Age-Appropriate Design Code status, state consumer-health-data acts) were not retrieved.
- Teen sleep-hour recommendations were not retrieved.

### Inferences
- **Positioning opportunity:** a direct-to-family product (one athlete plus a parent seat) fits a gap. B2B team tools charge per team or coach and give parents no role. A consumer price of roughly $10–$25 a month is anchored by CoachNow and TrainHeroic.
- **Anti-patterns to avoid:** heavy coach-side setup (the AI replaces it), Android parity issues, and completion depending on one tap.

### Gaps
- App Store and Reddit sentiment for Volt, TrainHeroic and BridgeAthletic was not retrieved (fetch blocked, searches returned aggregators).
- Hudl pricing and relevance were not researched.
- No parent-perspective reviews were found for any platform.
